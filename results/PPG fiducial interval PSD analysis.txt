The optimal fiducial points for pulse rate variability (PRV) analysis using photoplethysmography (PPG) signals include the middle-amplitude point (nM), apex point of the first derivative (nA), and tangent intersection point (nT). These points demonstrate the lowest relative errors between PRV and heart rate variability (HRV) indices, higher correlation coefficients, and improved reliability [1,12]. Forehead PPG signals were found to provide more reliable PRV assessments compared to finger PPG, likely due to differences in PPG morphology [12]. The medium interpolate point, a newly proposed fiducial point, also showed high accuracy under varying PPG morphologies and sensor locations, particularly when sampling rates were reduced to 50 Hz without significant loss of time and frequency domain indices [7].  

Power spectral density (PSD) analysis plays a critical role in evaluating PRV, as studies highlight its utility in assessing spectral characteristics of PPG-derived intervals. For example, spectral analysis techniques were employed to compare PRV indices with HRV, revealing that fiducial points like nM and nA significantly reduce errors in spectral estimates [5,7]. Additionally, the temporal intervals between fiducial points, such as the apex and foot of the PPG waveform, were analyzed using PSD to quantify variability, with the middle-amplitude point showing superior performance in maintaining accuracy across different sampling rates [7,9]. These findings underscore the importance of aligning fiducial point selection with spectral analysis to enhance PRV assessment reliability.  

The accuracy of PRV analysis also depends on algorithmic and signal processing factors. Studies emphasize that the choice of fiducial points and interbeat interval (IBI) detection algorithms significantly affects PRV results. For instance, valley-based detection methods for IBIs showed consistent performance across various spectral indices, while higher sampling rates (e.g., 128 Hz) improved PRV metrics like RMSSD and SD1/SD2 [7]. Furthermore, the use of derivative waveforms and clustering analysis helped identify 12 reliable markers for fiducial point detection, though their direct application to PSD analysis of intervals remains less explored [10]. These insights highlight the need for standardized protocols to ensure consistent PRV evaluation across diverse PPG acquisition settings.
